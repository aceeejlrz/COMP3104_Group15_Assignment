Why DevOps Matters in the SDLC
==============================

The SDLC in Short:
Software moves through planning, design, development, testing,
deployment and maintenance. Without DevOps, each stage is often owned
by a different team and work is handed over in large batches.

How DevOps Changes Each Stage:

1. Plan - Small, frequent releases mean plans can be adjusted based on
   real user feedback instead of guesses.

2. Develop - Short-lived branches and Pull Requests keep changes small
   and easy to review.

3. Test - Automated tests run in the CI pipeline on every push, so a
   bug is caught minutes after it is written, not weeks later.

4. Deploy - CD pipelines and containers make releases repeatable. A
   deployment becomes a routine step instead of a risky event.

5. Operate and Monitor - Logs, metrics and alerts show how the software
   behaves in production, and that information flows back into the
   next planning cycle.

Key Benefits:
- Shorter time from idea to release
- Fewer failed deployments and faster recovery when something breaks
- Shared responsibility between development and operations
- A documented, automated process that new team members can follow

Conclusion:
DevOps turns the SDLC from a one-way line into a continuous loop. Every
change is integrated, tested and delivered automatically, which lets a
team improve its software steadily and with confidence.
